# Documented Information Control Procedure

## 1. Purpose
Ensure documents and records required by the QMS are identifiable, reviewed, approved, available, protected and appropriately retained.

## 2. Responsibilities
- Document owner: drafts and reviews content for accuracy and relevance.
- Process owner: confirms operational suitability and implementation.
- Authorised approver: approves documents before issue.
- Document controller: maintains the master register, version status and controlled access.
- Users: use current approved documents and report errors or improvement needs.

## 3. Document identification
Each controlled document should have, as applicable:
- Unique title and document ID.
- Process or department owner.
- Version or revision number.
- Effective date and review date.
- Approval authority.
- Change history.

## 4. Creation and approval
1. Identify the need for a new or revised document.
2. Use an approved format and assign a unique ID.
3. Review content for accuracy, completeness and compatibility with related processes.
4. Obtain approval from the authorised role.
5. Record the approved version in the master document register.
6. Publish the controlled version in the approved repository.

## 5. Access and change control
- Restrict editing rights to authorised personnel.
- Make current versions available where needed.
- Record changes and revision status.
- Remove or clearly identify obsolete copies to prevent unintended use.
- Protect records from loss, unauthorised alteration or inappropriate disclosure.

## 6. External documents
Identify and control relevant external documents, such as customer specifications, legal requirements and standards, including their source and current status where practicable.

## 7. Retention and disposition
Define retention periods based on legal, contractual, operational and business requirements. Dispose of records securely when retention ends and no hold applies.

## 8. Minimum records
- Master document register.
- Document review and approval evidence.
- Revision history.
- Access and distribution records where needed.
- Obsolete-document disposition evidence where applicable.
