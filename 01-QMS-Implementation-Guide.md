# QMS Implementation and Maintenance Guide

## 1. Purpose
Provide a consistent approach for establishing, maintaining and improving a Quality Management System (QMS).

## 2. Understand the organisation
- Identify internal and external issues relevant to the organisation's purpose and strategic direction.
- Identify relevant interested parties and their applicable requirements.
- Determine the QMS scope, including boundaries, products and services.
- Identify processes, their sequence, interactions, owners, inputs, outputs and performance measures.

## 3. Leadership and planning
- Confirm leadership commitment, quality policy and assigned responsibilities.
- Establish measurable quality objectives at relevant functions and levels.
- Determine risks and opportunities that need to be addressed.
- Plan actions, resources, responsibilities, timelines and evaluation methods.

## 4. Support and documented information
- Determine people, infrastructure, environment and monitoring resources needed.
- Define competence requirements and retain appropriate evidence of competence.
- Promote awareness of the quality policy, relevant objectives and consequences of nonconformity.
- Control documented information, including identification, review, approval, access, storage, change control, retention and disposition.

## 5. Operational control
- Plan and control processes for products and services.
- Confirm customer requirements and address changes.
- Establish appropriate controls for externally provided processes, products and services.
- Define acceptance criteria and retain evidence that requirements have been met.
- Control nonconforming outputs to prevent unintended use or delivery.

## 6. Performance evaluation
Monitor relevant process measures and quality objectives. Analyse customer feedback, process performance, supplier performance, audit results and nonconformity trends. Conduct internal audits at planned intervals and management reviews at planned intervals.

## 7. Improvement
When a nonconformity occurs:
1. React to the issue and control or correct it.
2. Address consequences where appropriate.
3. Evaluate the need for action to eliminate the cause.
4. Implement action and review its effectiveness.
5. Update risks, opportunities or the QMS where necessary.

## 8. Suggested operating rhythm
- Weekly: review overdue actions and upcoming deadlines.
- Monthly: review objectives, training completion, supplier issues and process performance.
- Quarterly or risk-based: review audit programme progress and recurring trends.
- At planned intervals: conduct management review and assess QMS suitability, adequacy and effectiveness.

## 9. Evidence to retain
Examples include approved policies and procedures, competence records, audit programmes and reports, supplier evaluations, monitoring results, nonconformity records and management review outputs.

**Reminder:** This is a generic guide. Tailor it to the organisation's scope, risks, processes and applicable requirements.
